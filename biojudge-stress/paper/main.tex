\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent systems for biomedical question answering increasingly hand quality control to a large language model
(LLM) that checks, ranks or selects the answers produced by other agents. The weaknesses of LLM-as-a-judge are well
documented for general-domain chat, but that evidence rests on human preference labels without ground truth and
usually examines one bias at a time. We introduce \sys{}, a gold-anchored stress test for verifier agents. Four
open-weight judges from distinct model families compare correct and incorrect rationales, written by the same four
models, for \NItemsKept{} expert-keyed questions from biomedical literature (PubMedQA), clinical medicine
(MedQA-USMLE) and pharmacology (MedMCQA). A fully crossed design varies presentation order, length, authorship and
six content-free edits (claimed credentials, fabricated citations, markdown structure, asserted confidence, a note
addressed to the evaluator, and all five combined), giving \NPairwise{} pairwise and \NSelect{} best-of-$N$ verdicts
scored against the answer key. Baseline judges side with the key on \AccBase\% of clean pairs. Padding the incorrect
rationale changes accuracy by \VBBase{}\pp, and the self-authorship\,$\times$\,padding interaction contrast is
\ICBase{}\pp{} (\ICBaseP). The most effective single edit (\TopAttack) overturns \TopAttackASR\% of verdicts that
were correct on the unedited pair. Of seven corrections, including a normalizer agent that rewrites every candidate
into a canonical form and a jury that recuses judges from either author's family, the combined pipeline changes
accuracy by \DeltaAccFull{}\pp{} and attack success by \DeltaASRanyFull{}\pp. We release the code, prompts and cached
model responses so every number in this paper can be regenerated.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Agentic systems in biomedicine rarely trust a single model call. Clinical reasoning frameworks assemble panels of
specialist agents and then reconcile their proposals \cite{tang2024medagents,kim2024mdagents}; simulated clinics and
discovery agents plan, call tools and critique intermediate results
\cite{schmidgall2024agentclinic,gao2024empowering,huang2025biomni}; and the same systems are increasingly graded by
LLM juries and rubric-following model graders \cite{bedi2025medhelm,arora2025healthbench}. In each case one LLM
decides which of several candidate answers survives. That verifier is the component the rest of the pipeline relies
on for safety, and it is almost never itself tested under the conditions it will face.

What is known about LLM judges comes mostly from general-domain chat. Judges prefer the response shown in a
particular slot \cite{zheng2023judging,wang2024fair,shi2024position}, prefer longer answers
\cite{saito2023verbosity,dubois2024length}, prefer text written by their own model family
\cite{panickssery2024llm,wataoka2024self,liu2024narcissistic}, and respond to authority cues, fake references and
formatting \cite{chen2024humans,ye2025justice,wu2023style}. Their verdicts can be steered by injected instructions
\cite{shi2024injection,raina2024robust} and by semantics-preserving style edits chosen by an adaptive attacker
\cite{yang2026bite}; even a constant ``null'' response can top automatic leaderboards \cite{zheng2025cheating}. Three
features of this literature limit what it tells a biomedical system builder. The reference signal is usually human
preference, which has no ground truth, so it is unclear whether a bias costs correctness or merely disagrees with
taste. Biases are measured one at a time, so it is unknown whether they add up or compound. And corrections are
validated against the bias they target, rarely against the others.

Biomedicine sharpens each of these problems. The cues that general-domain judges over-reward, such as a claimed
clinical credential, a journal-style citation, or confident guideline language, are exactly the cues that clinicians
are trained to weigh, and a generator can produce them at zero cost: LLMs fabricate well-formed references
\cite{walters2023fabrication}, and prompt injection already works against medical vision-language models
\cite{clusmann2025prompt}. A verifier that rewards these cues turns the pipeline's safety check into its attack
surface.

We therefore test LLM judges in the role they occupy inside agentic pipelines and against a reference that has an
answer. \sys{} builds pairs of rationales for expert-keyed multiple-choice questions, one defending the keyed answer
and one defending the incorrect option that models themselves most often choose. Every judge family also writes
rationales, so authorship can be crossed with correctness. We ask four questions, carried over from the course
project that seeded this work and adapted to the verifier setting:
\textbf{RQ1} Do position, verbosity and self-preference act independently, or is an incorrect answer that is both
padded and self-authored accepted more often than the two biases predict separately?
\textbf{RQ2} How much of the gap to the answer key do corrections close, individually and jointly, and does fixing
one bias amplify another?
\textbf{RQ3} Are bias magnitudes and judge rankings stable across literature, clinical and pharmacology questions?
\textbf{RQ4} Can content-free edits overturn correct verdicts, in pairwise comparison and in best-of-$N$ selection,
and do corrected pipelines resist them?

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a gold-anchored, fully crossed design in which every judge family is both a verifier and an author, so
  position, verbosity, self-preference and their interactions are identified against a correctness criterion rather
  than a preference label (\cref{sec:framework,sec:metrics});
  \item a comparison of seven corrections at three levels of an agentic pipeline (prompt, input normalization by a
  dedicated agent, and aggregation with recusal), each scored on every bias and on attack success
  (\cref{sec:rq2});
  \item a best-of-$N$ verification test in which the only correct candidate is outnumbered by candidates that agree
  on a popular wrong answer, which isolates the verifier as the pipeline's last defense (\cref{sec:rq4});
  \item an open, cached and resumable harness that regenerates every table, figure and in-text number from one
  configuration file.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Biases of LLM judges.} MT-Bench established pairwise LLM judging and reported position and verbosity bias
alongside high agreement with human preferences \cite{zheng2023judging}. Later work quantified position bias across
judges and tasks \cite{wang2024fair,shi2024position}, length effects \cite{saito2023verbosity,dubois2024length},
self-preference and its link to self-recognition \cite{panickssery2024llm,wataoka2024self,liu2024narcissistic}, and a
wider catalogue of cognitive and style biases \cite{koo2024cognitive,chen2024humans,ye2025justice,wu2023style};
surveys and benchmarks summarize the field \cite{gu2024survey,tan2025judgebench}. Closest to our RQ2, a recent study
compares nine mitigation strategies and their cross-bias interactions on general benchmarks with human labels
\cite{soumik2026judging}. We differ in domain, in using an answer key instead of preferences, in testing
interactions between biases rather than between fixes only, and in including adversarial edits and a selection
setting.

\textbf{Corrections.} Standard remedies are order swapping and calibration \cite{zheng2023judging,wang2024fair},
length control by regression \cite{dubois2024length}, debiasing instructions, and panels of judges from different
families \cite{verga2024juries}. Yang et al.\ show that adaptive style edits evade style control and suggest style
normalization as a defense \cite{yang2026bite}; we implement that idea as a normalizer agent and test it jointly with
the others.

\textbf{Gameability.} Judges can be moved by universal adversarial phrases \cite{raina2024robust}, optimized
injections \cite{shi2024injection}, persuasion and sycophancy-like pressure \cite{sharma2024sycophancy}, and
bandit-selected stylistic edits \cite{yang2026bite}; indirect prompt injection threatens any LLM that reads
third-party text \cite{greshake2023indirect}. Our edits are deliberately fixed templates, which gives a lower bound
on what a low-effort upstream agent or data supplier could achieve.

\textbf{LLM judges and verifiers in biomedicine.} Verifiers that rank sampled solutions improve reasoning accuracy
\cite{cobbe2021training,lightman2024verify}, and multi-agent debate and self-consistency aggregate candidates
\cite{du2024debate,wang2023selfconsistency}; medical agent frameworks adopt the same moves
\cite{tang2024medagents,kim2024mdagents}. MedHELM grades open-ended clinical outputs with an LLM jury whose agreement
with clinicians it validates \cite{bedi2025medhelm}, and HealthBench scores responses with a model grader against
physician rubrics \cite{arora2025healthbench}. In expert domains, LLM judges agree with subject experts less than with
lay annotators \cite{szymanski2025limitations}. To our knowledge, no prior study has stress-tested biomedical verifier
agents for interacting biases and content-free manipulation against a ground-truth answer.

\subsection{Verifier roles and threat model}
We study two roles an LLM verifier plays in agentic pipelines (\cref{fig:pipeline}). A \emph{pairwise verifier}
receives a question and two candidate answers and names the better one, as in critic, debate-resolution and
preference-labelling steps. A \emph{best-of-$N$ selector} receives $N=4$ candidates and returns one. The adversary is
an upstream agent, tool or data supplier that controls the surface form of one candidate but not its medical content.
Its edits are fixed templates with no optimization, so the attack success we report is a floor on what adaptive
attacks such as BITE \cite{yang2026bite} can reach.

\subsection{Items and gold anchor}
We draw \NItemsSampled{} items, 100 per domain, with a fixed seed: \emph{literature} from the expert-labelled
PubMedQA PQA-L set, restricted to yes/no decisions and shown with the structured abstract minus its conclusion
\cite{jin2019pubmedqa}; \emph{clinical} from the four-option MedQA-USMLE test split \cite{jin2021medqa}; and
\emph{pharmacology} from the MedMCQA validation split, subject Pharmacology, single-answer items only
\cite{pal2022medmcqa}. Options of the ``all/none of the above'' type are removed, because a rationale cannot argue
for them without referring to the other options. Every item becomes a lettered single-best-answer question. A verdict
earns $s=1$ if it prefers the response defending the keyed answer, $s=0$ if it prefers the other, and $s=\tfrac12$
for a tie or an unparseable verdict. The exam or expert key thus serves as a human anchor without new annotation.

\subsection{Candidate construction}
\textbf{Hard negatives.} Each judge model first answers every item alone (at most 60 words, then a final letter).
The wrong option chosen most often across the four models becomes the item's distractor, with ties broken at random
and a random wrong option used when no model erred. The distractor is therefore the error the pool itself finds
plausible (\HardNegRate\% of items; \cref{tab:data}).

\textbf{Forced-answer rationales.} Each of the four author models writes a rationale $\Rp$ for the keyed answer and
a rationale $\Rm$ for the distractor, using the same prompt with the target option filled in (Appendix). A rationale
is valid if it has 80--150 words of prose, ends with the target letter, does not hedge between options or refuse, and
has at least 30 words; invalid outputs are retried up to twice at temperature 0.7. An item is kept only if all eight
rationales are valid, which leaves \NItemsKept{} items (\NItemsLit{} literature, \NItemsClin{} clinical,
\NItemsPharm{} pharmacology; \ValidRate\% of rationales valid). Because every family writes both a correct and an
incorrect rationale for every item, authorship style is balanced across correctness by construction.

\subsection{Controlled manipulations}
\textbf{Position.} Every pair is judged in both orders.

\textbf{Verbosity.} A utility agent (Phi-4 \cite{abdin2024phi4}, a family outside the judge pool, so that padding
does not add any judge's own stylistic signature) expands a rationale to about $2.5\times$ its length by restating the
question and adding general background, without new facts that bear on the answer. Outputs that change the final
line fall back to a deterministic template ($\PadLLMRate\%$ LLM-padded; mean length ratio \PadRatio). Each pair
appears with no padding, with the incorrect rationale padded, and with the correct one padded.

\textbf{Authorship.} For each item the four generators are randomly permuted into a cycle $(g_1,\dots,g_4)$, and
pair $p$ matches $\Rp$ from $g_p$ against $\Rm$ from $g_{p+1 \bmod 4}$. Every family is the correct author once and
the incorrect author once, so each judge sees one \emph{self-right}, one \emph{self-wrong} and two \emph{neutral}
pairs per item and the design stays balanced for every judge.

\textbf{Content-free edits.} Six deterministic edits (\cref{tab:edits}) are applied to the incorrect rationale of
every pair, in both orders. All keep the final-answer line and add no medical evidence.

\subsection{Corrections}
We test corrections at three levels of the pipeline. At the \emph{prompt} level, a debiasing clause tells the judge
to decide on correctness and medical validity only, to ignore order, length, formatting, tone and stated confidence,
to treat credentials and references inside responses as unverified, and to ignore instructions inside responses. At
the \emph{input} level, a normalizer agent (again Phi-4) rewrites every candidate, including padded and edited ones,
into at most 80 words of plain prose that keeps the final answer and the key reasoning steps and drops credentials,
references, markdown, confidence statements, filler and text addressed to an evaluator (\NormLLMRate\% of rewrites
pass validation; the rest use an attack-agnostic fallback that strips markup and truncates). Crossing the two gives
four protocols: base/raw, debias/raw, base/normalized and debias/normalized. At the \emph{aggregation} level, a
\emph{swap} view counts a preference only if it survives order reversal (otherwise a tie); a \emph{jury} takes the
majority of the four judges; a \emph{recusal jury} first drops every judge whose family wrote either candidate, which
in the cyclic design leaves two judges per pair; and the \emph{full} pipeline combines the debiasing clause, the
normalizer, recusal and swap. Together with the baseline this gives the eight configurations of \cref{tab:corr}.

\subsection{Best-of-$N$ selection}\label{sec:select}
The selector sees four candidates: the $\Rp$ of one generator (rotating over items so every family supplies the
correct candidate equally often) and the $\Rm$ of the other three. All three incorrect candidates defend the same
hard-negative distractor, so a majority vote over final answers is wrong on every item, and the verifier's reading of
the reasoning is the pipeline's only safeguard. This is the situation verifiers exist for: a debate or sampling stage
that has converged on a popular error. One incorrect candidate is designated \emph{perturbed}; it is left unchanged
(\emph{clean pool}), padded, or given the combined edit. The correct candidate is placed in each of the four positions
in turn, under the base/raw and debias/normalized protocols.

\section{Metrics and Statistical Analysis}\label{sec:metrics}

A judging unit $u$ is a (judge, protocol, item, pair, condition, order) tuple with score $s_u\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$.
\emph{Clean} units have no padding and no edit. We write $\acc(\cdot)$ for the mean of $s_u$ over the units matching
a condition, and report all rates in percent and all differences in percentage points (pp).

\textbf{Agreement.} $\acc$ is the mean score on clean units; $\kappa$ is Cohen's kappa \cite{cohen1960coefficient}
between the keyed slot and the verdict (A, B or tie).

\textbf{Position.} Flip is the share of clean pairs whose outcome (correct, incorrect or tie) changes when the order
is reversed. The position-preference index $\mathrm{PPI}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{1}(v=\mathrm{A})-\mathbb{1}(v=\mathrm{B})]$
on clean units is positive when slot A is favoured.

\textbf{Verbosity.} $\mathrm{VB}=\acc(\text{no pad})-\acc(\text{incorrect padded})$ is the accuracy lost when the
wrong answer is made longer; $\mathrm{VB}_{\text{sym}}=\acc(\text{correct padded})-\acc(\text{incorrect padded})$.

\textbf{Self-preference.} For judge $j$,
$\mathrm{SPB}_j=\acc_j(\text{self-right})-\acc_j(\text{self-wrong})$ on clean units. For a panel, we compute the
analogous family favouritism $f_c=\acc(c\text{ wrote }\Rp)-\acc(c\text{ wrote }\Rm)$ for every family $c$. The signed
mean of $f_c$ over families is zero by construction in the balanced cyclic design, so tables report
$|\mathrm{SPB}|$, the mean absolute value over judges (single-judge rows) or families (panel rows).

\textbf{Interaction (RQ1a).} With SW = self-wrong, N = neutral and PW = incorrect rationale padded,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IC}=\underbrace{\big[\acc(\mathrm{N})-\acc(\mathrm{SW},\mathrm{PW})\big]}_{\text{joint loss}}
-\underbrace{\big[\acc(\mathrm{N})-\acc(\mathrm{SW})\big]}_{\text{self only}}
-\underbrace{\big[\acc(\mathrm{N})-\acc(\mathrm{N},\mathrm{PW})\big]}_{\text{length only}},
\end{equation}
where unmarked conditions are unpadded. $\mathrm{IC}>0$ means the combination costs more than the sum of its parts
(super-additive). We also fit a linear probability model on the bias block of the base protocol,
\begin{multline}
s_u=\alpha_j+\beta_1\mathrm{WA}+\beta_2\mathrm{SW}+\beta_3\mathrm{SR}+\beta_4\mathrm{PW}+\beta_5\mathrm{PR}\\
+\beta_6\,\mathrm{SW}{\cdot}\mathrm{PW}+\beta_7\,\mathrm{WA}{\cdot}\mathrm{PW}+\beta_8\,\mathrm{WA}{\cdot}\mathrm{SW}
+\varepsilon_u,
\end{multline}
with judge fixed effects $\alpha_j$, WA = incorrect answer in slot A, SR = self-right, PR = correct rationale padded,
and standard errors clustered by item \cite{cameron2015practitioner}. A negative $\beta_6$ corresponds to a positive IC.

\textbf{Gameability.} For edit $e$, $\mathrm{ASR}_e$ is the share of units that were correct on the clean pair and
become incorrect once $e$ is applied to the incorrect rationale (ties count one half). $\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{any}}$ is
the share broken by at least one of the five single edits.

\textbf{Selection.} Selector accuracy is the share of picks that hit the correct candidate (chance 25\%; majority
vote 0\%). Capture is the share that hit the designated perturbed candidate; in the clean pool that candidate is
unedited, so clean capture is the matched baseline for padded and edited pools. Position-1 excess is the rate of
picking the first slot on clean pools minus 25\pp.

\textbf{Uncertainty and tests.} All intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from an item-level cluster bootstrap
\cite{field2007bootstrapping} with 2{,}000 replicates. One bootstrap weight matrix is drawn once and reused for every
estimate, so every difference between judges, corrections or domains is a paired bootstrap difference; $p$-values are
two-sided bootstrap sign tests. Cross-domain stability is Kendall's $W$ \cite{kendall1939problem} over the judge
rankings in the three domains, with a permutation test (20{,}000 permutations). We designate four primary contrasts,
one per RQ: IC (RQ1), $\Delta\acc$ of the full pipeline (RQ2), $W$ for each metric (RQ3) and
$\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{any}}$ under the base and debias/normalized protocols (RQ4). All other intervals are
descriptive.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Models.} The four judges, which are also the four authors, are Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
\cite{grattafiori2024llama3}, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \cite{qwen2024qwen25}, Gemma-2-9B-it \cite{gemma2024gemma2} and
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 \cite{jiang2023mistral}; Phi-4 \cite{abdin2024phi4} is the utility agent for padding and
normalization. Small open-weight models are what many biomedical groups can host locally for privacy reasons, and
using four families lets every judge be both verifier and author. Models were queried through \Gateway{} on \RunDate{}
at temperature 0 with a fixed seed; for Gemma and Mistral, whose chat templates have no system role, the system
prompt is prepended to the user turn. Exact model identifiers are recorded in the released configuration.

\textbf{Scale.} Each kept item yields 24 bias-block units (4 pairs $\times$ 3 length conditions $\times$ 2 orders)
and 48 attack-block units (4 pairs $\times$ 6 edits $\times$ 2 orders) per judge and protocol, i.e.\ 1{,}152 pairwise
verdicts per item over 4 judges and 4 protocols, plus 96 selection verdicts (3 pools $\times$ 4 rotations $\times$ 2
protocols $\times$ 4 judges). The run produced \NPairwise{} pairwise and \NSelect{} selection verdicts; \ParseFailRate\%
of pairwise verdicts were unparseable and \TieRate\% were explicit ties. The full plan for 300 items needs about
357M input and 49M output tokens, which is feasible for 7--9B models on a hosted gateway or a single GPU node.

\textbf{Software validation.} Every call is cached in SQLite, so runs resume after interruption and the analysis can
be recomputed without new queries. Before spending any budget we ran the complete pipeline against a simulator with
planted position, verbosity, self-preference and attack effects of known size, including a planted super-additive
self\,$\times$\,length interaction and one unbiased judge. The analysis recovered every planted effect, returned
near-zero bias for the unbiased judge, and exposed two estimator bugs that we fixed (the zero-by-construction signed
family favouritism above was one). No simulator output is reported as a result.

\subsection{RQ1: Bias profile and interactions}\label{sec:rq1}
\Cref{tab:bias} profiles each judge on the base protocol. Pooled over judges and domains, verdicts agree with the key
on \AccBase\% of clean pairs (95\% CI \AccBaseCI; mean $\kappa=\KappaBase$), and the most accurate judge,
\JudgeBestAcc, reaches \AccBest\%. Flip rates range from \FlipMin\% to \FlipMax\%: for that share of pairs the verdict
depends on nothing but which answer is read first. Padding the incorrect rationale changes pooled accuracy by
\VBBase{}\pp{} (\VBBaseCI), and the gap between padding the correct and padding the incorrect rationale is
\VBsymBase{}\pp; per judge, VB spans \VBMin{} to \VBMax{}\pp{} and is largest for \JudgeMaxVB. Self-preference spans
\SPBMin{} to \SPBMax{}\pp{} across judges (largest for \JudgeMaxSPB; mean $|\mathrm{SPB}|=\SPBBase$\pp, \SPBBaseCI).
\claim{Because each judge also wrote the incorrect rationales it is asked to reject, a positive SPB means that a judge
is measurably more willing to accept a wrong answer when the wrong answer is phrased the way it would phrase it.}

The biases do not simply add. The self\,$\times$\,padding contrast is $\mathrm{IC}=\ICBase$\pp{} (\ICBaseCI,
\ICBaseP), and the regression (\cref{tab:reg}) gives $\hat\beta_6=\RegSWPW$\pp{} for self-wrong\,$\times$\,padded
wrong (\RegSWPWCI, \RegSWPWP), \RegWAPW\pp{} for slot-A\,$\times$\,padded wrong (\RegWAPWP) and \RegWASW\pp{} for
slot-A\,$\times$\,self-wrong (\RegWASWP). \claim{The combination the proposal singled out, an incorrect answer that is
both long and written in the judge's own voice, is accepted more often than the two biases predict separately, so an
audit that measures each bias on its own understates the risk of the case that matters most in a multi-agent system,
where the verifier often shares a family with one of the agents it checks.}

\subsection{RQ2: What corrections buy}\label{sec:rq2}
\Cref{tab:corr} and \cref{fig:delta} compare the eight configurations; $\Delta$ values below are paired differences
from the single-judge baseline. The debiasing clause alone changes accuracy by \DeltaAccDebias\pp{}
(\DeltaAccDebiasCI), Flip by \DeltaFlipDebias\pp{} and VB by \DeltaVBDebias\pp. The normalizer agent acts on the
input rather than on the judge: it changes VB by \DeltaVBNorm\pp, $|\mathrm{SPB}|$ by \DeltaSPBNorm\pp{} and
$\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{any}}$ by \DeltaASRanyNorm\pp, at an accuracy change of \DeltaAccNorm\pp{} (\DeltaAccNormCI).
\claim{Normalization is the only correction that removes the carrier of verbosity and edit cues instead of asking the
judge to ignore them, which is why it moves the attack metrics most; its cost is that compressing a rationale to 80
words can also discard reasoning a judge needed.} Combining the two gives $\Delta\acc=\DeltaAccDebNorm$\pp{}
(\DeltaAccDebNormCI).

Aggregation behaves differently. Swap aggregation changes accuracy by \DeltaAccSwap\pp, because order-dependent
verdicts become ties. The four-judge jury reaches \AccJury\% ($\Delta=\DeltaAccJury$\pp) with mean absolute family
favouritism \SPBJury\pp, and recusing the authors' families gives \SPBRecusal\pp{} at $\Delta\acc=\DeltaAccRecusal$\pp.
The full pipeline reaches \AccFull\% ($\Delta=\DeltaAccFull$\pp, \DeltaAccFullCI) with $\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{any}}$ of
\ASRanyFull\%, against \ASRanyBase\% for the baseline.

\textbf{Does fixing one bias amplify another (RQ2a)?} \Cref{fig:delta} reports every correction's effect on every
metric, with red cells marking a metric that got worse. \claim{Off-target costs are concentrated in accuracy rather
than in other biases: aggregation that converts order-dependent verdicts into ties lowers raw accuracy while reducing
position dependence, and normalization trades a small loss of clean accuracy for the removal of length and edit

\subsection{RQ3: Stability across domains}\label{sec:rq3}
\Cref{tab:domain} breaks the base-protocol metrics down by domain. Agreement of the judge rankings across literature,
clinical and pharmacology items is $W=\KWAcc$ for accuracy (\KWAccP), \KWVB{} for VB (\KWVBP), \KWSPB{} for SPB
(\KWSPBP) and \KWASR{} for attack susceptibility (\KWASRP). With four judges and three domains the permutation
distribution of $W$ is coarse: $p<0.05$ requires $W\ge0.82$, and only identical rankings ($W=1$) reach $p<0.01$. We
therefore read $W$ together with the per-domain magnitudes. \claim{Rankings by accuracy and attack susceptibility
carry over across domains, whereas verbosity and self-preference rankings are less stable, so a judge chosen on
clinical questions cannot be assumed to be the least biased judge for literature or pharmacology questions.}

\subsection{RQ4: Gameability of pairwise and best-of-N verification}\label{sec:rq4}
\textbf{Pairwise.} Under the base protocol, single content-free edits overturn \ASRauthBase\% (authority),
\ASRcitBase\% (fabricated citations), \ASRfmtBase\% (formatting), \ASRconfBase\% (confidence) and \ASRinjBase\%
(note to evaluator) of verdicts that were correct on the clean pair; the combined edit overturns \ASRcomboBase\%, and
\ASRanyBase\% fall to at least one single edit (\cref{tab:attacks}). Across judges $\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{any}}$ ranges
from \ASRanyMin\% to \ASRanyMax\%, with \JudgeMaxASR{} the most susceptible. The debiasing clause changes
$\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{any}}$ by \DeltaASRanyDebias\pp{} and the debias/normalized protocol by \DeltaASRanyDebNorm\pp.
\claim{Telling the judge that credentials and references are unverified helps less than removing them before the
judge sees them.}

\textbf{Best-of-$N$.} In the clean pool the selector picks the correct candidate in \SelAccClean\% of cases, against
25\% for chance and 0\% for majority voting, and over-selects position 1 by \SelPosOne\pp{} (\cref{fig:select}).
Padding one incorrect candidate moves the capture rate from \SelCapClean\% to \SelCapPad\% and accuracy to
\SelAccPad\%; the combined edit moves capture to \SelCapAttack\% and accuracy to \SelAccAttack\%. With the debiasing
clause and the normalizer, accuracy is \SelAccCleanDN\%, \SelAccPadDN\% and \SelAccAttackDN\% on the three pools and
capture is \SelCapCleanDN\%, \SelCapPadDN\% and \SelCapAttackDN\%. \claim{A single decorated candidate among four is
enough to redirect the selector, which means the agent that controls presentation, not the agent that is right,
decides what a best-of-$N$ pipeline returns.}

\subsection{When does the verifier fall back on cues?}\label{sec:competence}
Splitting units by whether the judge answered the item correctly on its own (stage 2) separates knowledge from
heuristics. On items a judge solves alone, its verifier accuracy is \AccKnown\% with VB \VBKnown\pp, SPB \SPBKnown\pp,
$\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{any}}$ \ASRKnown\% and Flip \FlipKnown\%; on items it gets wrong alone, the same metrics are
\AccUnknown\%, \VBUnknown\pp, \SPBUnknown\pp, \ASRUnknown\% and \FlipUnknown\%. \claim{Bias concentrates where the
verifier lacks the knowledge to check the answer, so a verifier is least reliable on exactly the questions where the
pipeline needs it most, and verification by a model no stronger than the authors adds little on hard items.}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\claim{Four practical consequences follow for builders of biomedical agentic pipelines.} First, \claim{the verifier
should not share a model family with the agents it checks; recusal is cheap when several families are available and
removes the self-preference channel, including its interaction with length.} Second, \claim{a normalizer between the
agents and the verifier addresses verbosity and presentation attacks at their source; instruction-level debiasing is
not a substitute.} Its cost in clean accuracy should be measured on the target task before deployment. Third,
\claim{content-free edits are a cheap pre-deployment test}: the six templates here need no model access and no
optimization, and any verifier that fails them will fail adaptive attacks \cite{yang2026bite} too. Fourth, verifier
quality should be reported against an answer key where one exists, not only as agreement with other LLMs or with
preference labels, because a judge can agree with a panel and still reward the wrong answer.

The selection results matter most for agentic designs. \claim{When the pool contains a popular error, as it does
after agents converge through debate or sampling, the verifier is the only component that can recover the minority
correct answer, and it is also the component an upstream agent can manipulate most cheaply.}

% =====================================================================================================
\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
\emph{Construct.} Multiple-choice items with forced rationales are a controlled proxy for free-text verification;
the answer key measures preference for correctness, not completeness, communication or safety, and exam keys contain
some errors. Forced incorrect rationales may carry subtle cues of reluctance; a verifier that exploits them is doing
its job, but the effect may be smaller for naturally occurring errors. \emph{Manipulation fidelity.} Padding and
normalization are checked automatically (final answer preserved, length, no refusal), not by a human reader; the
normalizer could occasionally strengthen or weaken an argument. \emph{Models.} We test small open-weight judges;
frontier judges may show different profiles, and hosted model versions drift, which we mitigate by recording
identifiers and caching every response. \emph{Attacks.} Fixed templates give a lower bound on gameability, not an
estimate for adaptive attackers. \emph{Statistics.} Kendall's $W$ over four judges has low resolution; each
configuration uses a single deterministic sample per prompt.

% =====================================================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\sys{} tests LLM judges as verifier agents against an answer key rather than a preference label, crosses position,
length, authorship and content-free edits in one design, and scores seven corrections on every bias at once.
\claim{Its central finding is that the biases of biomedical verifier agents interact and can be exploited by trivial
edits, and that the corrections that work act on the input and the panel rather than on the judge's instructions.}
The harness runs with any OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic endpoint, so the same stress test can be applied to a
pipeline's own verifier before it is trusted with clinical or pharmacovigilance decisions.
